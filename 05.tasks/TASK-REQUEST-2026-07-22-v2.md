## The work, in priority order

### 1. Cut 20 figures and tables down to 10 (blocking, and the biggest job)

Springer caps original articles at **ten figures and tables in total**. The draft has
eleven tables and nine figures. This is a scientific decision about what the paper must
show, not a formatting exercise, so it needs the author.

Some obvious candidates to consider, not decide. `@tbl-coef` is four rows of published
coefficients and could become a sentence. `@tbl-normal` is diagnostic and is a natural
supplement. `@tbl-descr` and `@tbl-vri` are both sample descriptions and might merge.
`@fig-f1` through `@fig-f4` are all Stage 1 diagnostics and could combine into one
multi-panel figure. `@tbl-affleck` and `@tbl-three` are both Stage 2b and could merge.

Note that the old memo's length target is dead. The DellaSala exemplar sets ~8,000 words
with 7 tables and 7 figures, which is 14 floats and still over the Springer cap, so the
exemplar can no longer set the float count. **Do not add words or floats to hit an old
target.** At ~8,900 words including references the draft is comfortably inside the 10,000
limit; the constraint that binds is the float count.

Springer requires 150 to 250 words. The current abstract is **431** and reads as a summary

### 5. Fix Figure 9b, which currently overstates what it shows

`02.inputs/scripts/stage2b_affleck_comparator.py:156-164`, plotting grid at line 140, height
relation at lines 86-87. Neither curve is data. `refit/national` reduces exactly to
`3.076 d^-0.1855`. `Affleck/national` reduces to about `0.266 d^0.468` once the fitted
height relation `h = 1.737 d^0.783` is substituted. The panel is two smooth power curves.

Three specific defects:

- **The caption is false.** It says both equations exceed the national coefficient across
  the operational range. Affleck sits **below** it until 17.8 cm and reads 0.60 at 6 cm.
  The Results prose is careful and starts at 20 cm; the caption is not.
- **The grid starts at 6 cm.** VRI western-larch stands run **13.9 to 111.0 cm** QMD, so
  the left third of the panel describes stands that do not exist in the inventory.
- **Panel (b) shades nothing**, while panel (a) shades its extrapolation, so the least
  defensible part of the figure carries the same visual authority as the rest.

The crossing at 43.9 cm is real and the caption's "near 45 cm" is correct. Suggested fix:
start the grid at the inventory minimum or shade below it, shade beyond the 19.8 cm refit
calibration ceiling as panel (a) does, mark where Affleck crosses unity, and rewrite the
caption to say Affleck exceeds the national coefficient above about 18 cm.

### 6. Reconcile the stem-bark component definitions (largest remaining uncertainty)

Unchanged from the previous memo and still the most valuable unblocked science. The 37 to 60
percent bracket is wide mostly because the two western-larch equations define stem bark
differently: Affleck runs from a 30 cm stump to a 5 cm top, while ENFOR and LegacyTreeData
include the stump and run to the tip. Our equation is more than twice Affleck's at 10 cm and
crosses below it by 45 cm, a pattern consistent with that truncation.

Cheapest route first: recompute our own equation on a stump-to-5 cm-top basis using the
LegacyTreeData section data already in the repository (`02.inputs/legacy-tree/section.txt`,
3.2 MB, in git). That makes the two directly comparable with no new data. Failing that,
request section-level data from Affleck; the draft in `affleck-data-request-draft.md` has
been rewritten for this narrower purpose.

### 7. Read Ung et al. 2008

Now more load-bearing than when it was first listed. Lambert et al. 2005 **has been read**
and its claims are verified in the manuscript: 8,636 trees across 33 species, tamarack
contributing 575 trees over 1.8 to 44.5 cm, and the tamarack bark coefficients matching the
paper digit for digit. But the **generic conifer coefficient the whole western-larch argument
rests on is not Lambert's**: his Softwood bark is a = 0.0162, b = 2.1959, where the
operational table carries a = 0.0153, b = 2.2110. That is Ung's re-estimate, and it is
unread. CJFR 38:1123-1132, doi 10.1139/X07-224. Institutional access needed.

Note the filing trap: `04.references/literature/Ung & Lambert 2005 National Canadian
allometrics.pdf` **is Lambert, Ung & Raulier 2005**, not an Ung paper. Ung et al. 2008 is not
in the repository.
