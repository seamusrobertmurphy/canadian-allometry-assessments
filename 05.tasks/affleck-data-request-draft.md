# Data request draft: Affleck / INGY western-larch destructive dataset

Prepared 2026-07-21. Send from the author's institutional address. Not yet sent.

**To:** Dr David Affleck, W.A. Franke College of Forestry and Conservation, University of
Montana (Inland Northwest Growth and Yield Cooperative)

**What we are asking for:** the western larch (*Larix occidentalis*) destructive stem
measurements underlying Affleck and Diéguez-Aranda (2016) and Affleck (2018, *Forest Ecology
and Management* 432: 179-188), specifically stem-bark mass or paired inside- and
outside-bark diameters by section, with DBH and total height, for the roughly 470 trees
reported to 105 cm DBH.

**Rationale revised 2026-07-21.** The original reason for this request has been overtaken:
Affleck (2019) *publishes* the LAOC stembark equation, m_b = 0.001910 d^1.636 h^1.332, and
we have applied it as an independent large-tree comparator (Stage 2b). The paper no longer
depends on obtaining the raw data.

**What the raw data would still resolve:** the component-definition gap, which is now the
largest single source of the remaining uncertainty. Affleck's stembark runs from a 30 cm
stump to a 5 cm top; ENFOR and LegacyTreeData include the stump and run to the tip. Our
equation and Affleck's differ by a factor of 2.6 at 10 cm and converge and cross by 45 cm,
a pattern consistent with that truncation. Section-level data would let us recompute
Affleck's trees on a whole-stem basis and collapse the 37 to 60 percent provincial bracket
towards a point estimate. This is now a narrowing request, not a load-bearing one, and the
priority is correspondingly lower.

## Draft message

Subject: Request for western larch destructive stem-bark data (INGY), for a national
allometric assessment

Dear Dr Affleck,

I am completing a component-level assessment of Canada's national biomass equations
(Lambert et al. 2005; Ung et al. 2008) for stem bark in the larches, and a province-wide
application of the results to the British Columbia Vegetation Resources Inventory.

Western larch has no coefficient in the national system and is estimated through the generic
coniferous coefficient. On open destructive data that coefficient underestimates western
larch stem bark by about 47 percent, while the same generic coefficient over-predicts
tamarack, whose own coefficient is near unbiased. The two larches diverge, and independent
stem-taper measurements put bark at 24 percent of the stem cross-section in western larch
against 13 percent in tamarack, with bark area scaling above isometry in the one species and
below it in the other.

The limitation is size. The open western-larch bark-mass data reach only 19.8 cm DBH, and
the taper geometry 39.4 cm, whereas the inventory stands we apply the equations to average
32 cm quadratic mean diameter and extend well beyond. Our provincial estimate is therefore
an extrapolation, and its uncertainty interval is correspondingly wide.

Your INGY western-larch destructive sample, reported to 105 cm DBH, would replace that
extrapolation with measurement. If those data can be shared, I would be glad to discuss
terms, including co-authorship if you would find that appropriate, and I would of course
follow whatever citation, acknowledgement, and data-use conditions the cooperative requires.
Bark mass by tree would be ideal; paired inside- and outside-bark section diameters would
serve equally well.

I am happy to send the current manuscript so you can see exactly how the data would be used.
